Describing Myself

Personality Traits · Adjective & Adverb Intensifiers · Interview English

📋 Vocabulary: Personality Traits

When describing yourself in a professional setting, choose words that accurately reflect your working style and social interactions.

Reserved: Keeps feelings hidden; thinks before speaking.
Sociable: Enjoys being with others; friendly.
Assertive: Confident and direct in expressing ideas.
Easygoing: Relaxed and tolerant; easy to work with.
Strict: Demands that rules concerning behavior are obeyed.
Outgoing: Socially confident and highly interactive.
Optimistic: Hopeful and confident about the future.
Pessimistic: Tending to see the worst aspect of things.

📈 Language Focus: Intensifiers

Intensifiers are words placed before adjectives or adverbs to modify their strength. In an interview, using them makes your descriptions sound much more natural and precise.

Strong Intensifiers (very, so, really)

Used to emphasize a strong characteristic.

  • "I am a very outgoing person."
  • "She works really hard under pressure."
Moderate Intensifiers (pretty, quite, rather, fairly)

Used to soften a characteristic, often used with negative traits to sound more professional.

  • "I can be quite shy at first."
  • "He is a rather reserved manager."

💬 Communication: Useful Interview Structures

Avoid simply saying "I am happy" or "I am strict." Use full conversational structures to sound professional.

  • Describing yourself: "I consider myself to be quite sociable."
  • Describing a colleague: "What stands out most about him is that he is very easygoing."
  • Adding important details: "One thing you should know about me is that I am highly organized."
  • Summarizing: "If I had to describe myself in one word, that would be assertive."
