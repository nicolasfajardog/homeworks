📌 Grammar Notes: Conditionals & Certainty

Conditionals Summary:

  • Zero (Natural/General Truths): If + Present Simple, Present Simple. (If we order in bulk, suppliers give us a discount.)
  • First (Real Future Possibility): If + Present Simple, Will + Base Verb. (If prices rise next month, we will freeze our purchase order.)
  • Second (Unreal Present/Future): If + Past Simple, Would + Base Verb. (If I had full autonomy, I would change our primary distributor.)
  • Third (Unreal Past/Regrets): If + Past Perfect, Would Have + Participle. (If we had signed the agreement in January, we would have saved $50,000.)

Adverbs of Likelihood: certainly (100%), definitely (90%), probably (80%), likely (70%), possibly (50%), unlikely (30%), not really (10%), not at all (0%). Placement rule: place the adverb before the main verb (e.g., They will probably accept the deal).

Case Study: Global Wholesale Supply Chain Decision
Last quarter, Apex Wholesale faced a major logistics crisis. If the procurement team had conducted a risk assessment in January, they would have noticed that their primary overseas manufacturer was struggling with raw material shortages. Because they didn't, the shipment was delayed by six weeks, resulting in a 15% loss in regional sales.

Now, the head buyer is revising the procurement strategy: "If we diversify our suppliers across South America this month, we will definitely reduce lead times by 20%. Furthermore, if I were to choose a new primary distributor, I would probably prioritize reliability over the absolute lowest price."
  1. 1. Why did Apex Wholesale experience a six-week delay last quarter?
    A They chose air freight instead of sea shipping.
    B They didn't conduct a risk assessment early, missing the manufacturer's supply issue.
    C South American suppliers canceled their contracts without notice.
  2. 2. What is the predicted consequence of supplier diversification this month?
    A Lead times will definitely decrease by 20%.
    B Regional sales will probably fall another 15%.
    C Wholesale prices will unlikely stay stable.
  3. 3. What would the head buyer do if selecting a new distributor now?
    A Always select the absolute cheapest price available.
    B Stop buying from overseas manufacturers altogether.
    C Probably place a higher priority on vendor reliability than cost alone.
